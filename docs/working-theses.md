# Working theses and their limits

These are testable research positions, not established universal laws.

## T1 — No semantic sovereignty

Representing a successful transition does not cause it. Descriptions must be grounded in feasible interfaces and execution mechanisms. A programming-language grammar can be changed by changing a compiler; laws of physics are not altered by renaming an instruction.

**Failure test:** show an apparently valid high-level plan whose concrete dependency is absent.

## T2 — Capability is configuration-relative

What an agent can realize depends on its environment, resources, embodiment, rights, interfaces, and co-operating systems. A capability may become reachable through a tool connection without being implemented inside the agent.

**Failure test:** keep the agent fixed and vary access topology, permissions, or cost.

## T3 — Reconfiguration alters future feasible actions

Adding, removing, replicating, relocating, or reconnecting a component can change which tasks an agent can subsequently realize.

**Failure test:** a topology edit that appears beneficial but fails because of budget or dependencies.

## T4 — Observed success does not establish necessity

A system's successful operation with a node does not prove the node is indispensable; operation without the node does not prove it offered no resilience. Evaluations need counterfactual scenarios, including disturbances.

**Failure test:** a spare provider whose value appears only under a site outage.

## T5 — Models must remain revisable

The model's categories, links, and requirements may be wrong. Observed residuals justify investigation but do not uniquely determine a causal correction.

**Failure test:** two different unmodeled mechanisms producing the same observed discrepancy.

## T6 — Accumulation is conditional

Retention, modularity, and reuse can make capabilities compound over time, but maintenance, coordination, path dependence, and resource costs may dominate the gains.

**Failure test:** introducing a reusable abstraction that increases fragility or overhead enough to make performance worse.

## Interpreting the framework

The framework is descriptive and instrumental. It neither proves that persistence is always an agent's ultimate objective nor entails that increased autonomy, decentralization, or scale is intrinsically desirable.
